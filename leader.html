NATIONAL LEADERS

MAHATMA GANDHI

Mahatma Gandhi, also known as “Bapu” and the Father of the Nation, was born on 2 October 1869 in Porbandar, Gujarat. His father was Karamchand Gandhi and his mother was Putlibai. He studied law in London and later worked in South Africa. Gandhi led India’s freedom struggle through truth and nonviolence. He started major movements like the Non-Cooperation Movement, Dandi March, and Quit India Movement. His simple lifestyle and strong morals united millions of Indians and played a decisive role in achieving independence in 1947.

PT. JAWAHARLAL NEHRU

Jawaharlal Nehru, popularly known as “Chacha Nehru”, was born on 14 November 1889 in Allahabad. His father was Motilal Nehru and his mother was Swarup Rani. He studied law in England. Nehru actively participated in the freedom movement under Mahatma Gandhi and spent many years in prison. After independence, he became the first Prime Minister of India. He focused on democracy, education, science, and industrial development, laying the foundation of modern India.

SUBHAS CHANDRA BOSE

Subhas Chandra Bose, respectfully called “Netaji”, was born on 23 January 1897 in Cuttack, Odisha. His father was Janakinath Bose and his mother was Prabhavati Devi. He was highly educated and cleared the Indian Civil Services exam. Bose believed freedom could be achieved through armed struggle. He formed the Azad Hind Fauj and inspired people with the slogan “Give me blood, and I will give you freedom.” His courage and patriotism left a lasting impact.

SARDAR VALLABHBHAI PATEL

Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, known as the “Iron Man of India”, was born on 31 October 1875 in Nadiad, Gujarat. His father was Jhaverbhai Patel and his mother was Ladbai. He studied law in England. Patel played an important role in the freedom struggle and led several farmer movements. After independence, he united more than 500 princely states into one nation. His strong leadership ensured India’s unity and integrity.

BHAGAT SINGH

Bhagat Singh, remembered as “Shaheed Bhagat Singh”, was born on 28 September 1907 in Banga, Punjab. His father was Kishan Singh and his mother was Vidyavati. He studied at DAV College, Lahore. He was a revolutionary who believed in fearless action against British injustice. He threw non-lethal bombs in the Central Assembly to protest colonial laws. He was executed at a young age, becoming a powerful symbol of sacrifice and bravery.

CHANDRASHEKHAR AZAD

Chandrashekhar Azad, famously known as “Azad”, was born on 23 July 1906 in Bhavra, Madhya Pradesh. His father was Sitaram Tiwari and his mother was Jagrani Devi. He joined the revolutionary movement at a young age. Azad was a key leader of the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association. He believed in armed resistance and never wanted to be captured alive. He sacrificed his life while fighting British forces.

DR. BHIMRAO AMBEDKAR

Dr. Bhimrao Ambedkar, respectfully called “Babasaheb Ambedkar”, was born on 14 April 1891 in Mhow, Madhya Pradesh. His father was Ramji Sakpal and his mother was Bhimabai. He received higher education in India and abroad. Ambedkar fought against social discrimination and inequality. He was the chief architect of the Indian Constitution and ensured justice, liberty, and equality for all citizens of independent India.

RABINDRANATH TAGORE

Rabindranath Tagore, respectfully known as “Gurudev”, was born on 7 May 1861 in Kolkata, West Bengal. His father was Debendranath Tagore and his mother was Sarada Devi. He received education both in India and abroad. Tagore was a poet, writer, philosopher, and freedom supporter. Though he did not take part in active politics, his writings inspired national pride and unity. He composed India’s national anthem “Jana Gana Mana” and used literature to awaken the spirit of freedom among Indians.

LALA LAJPAT RAI

Lala Lajpat Rai, popularly known as “Punjab Kesari”, was born on 28 January 1865 in Punjab. His father was Munshi Radha Krishna and his mother was Gulab Devi. He studied law and became a national leader. He was part of the Lal-Bal-Pal trio. He strongly protested against the Simon Commission and suffered fatal injuries during a protest. His sacrifice made him a martyr of India’s freedom struggle.

BAL GANGADHAR TILAK

Bal Gangadhar Tilak, widely known as “Lokmanya Tilak”, was born on 23 July 1856 in Ratnagiri, Maharashtra. His father was Gangadhar Tilak and his mother was Parvati Bai. He was a scholar, teacher, and journalist. Tilak inspired nationalism among Indians. He gave the famous slogan “Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it.” His leadership strengthened the demand for complete independence.

RANI LAKSHMIBAI

Rani Lakshmibai, also known as the Queen of Jhansi, was born on 30 November 1827 in Varanasi. Her father was Moropant Tambe and her mother was Bhagirathi Bai. She was trained in horse riding and sword fighting. She played a heroic role in the Revolt of 1857 and fought bravely against British forces. Her courage and sacrifice made her a symbol of women’s power and patriotism.

MANGAL PANDEY

Mangal Pandey was born on 19 July 1827 in Ballia, Uttar Pradesh. He served as a soldier in the British East India Company’s army. He was among the first to openly rebel against British rule. His actions sparked the Revolt of 1857. He was executed by the British, but his bravery inspired widespread resistance and marked the beginning of organized struggle against colonial rule.

MAULANA ABUL KALAM

Maulana Abul Kalam Azad was born on 11 November 1888 in Mecca. His father was Maulana Khairuddin and his mother was Alia Begum. He was a scholar, writer, and freedom fighter. Azad actively participated in the Non-Cooperation Movement and promoted Hindu-Muslim unity. After independence, he became India’s first Education Minister and worked to strengthen the education system of the nation.

RAM PRASAD BISMIL

Ram Prasad Bismil was born on 11 June 1897 in Shahjahanpur, Uttar Pradesh. His father was Murlidhar and his mother was Mulmati. He was a revolutionary and poet. Bismil played a major role in the Kakori Train Conspiracy against British rule. He fought bravely for independence and was executed by the British. His sacrifice inspired generations of freedom fighters.

ASHFAQULLA KHAN

Ashfaqulla Khan was born on 22 October 1900 in Shahjahanpur, Uttar Pradesh. His father was Shafiq Ullah Khan and his mother was Mazhar-un-Nissa. He was a close associate of Ram Prasad Bismil. He took part in the Kakori conspiracy and strongly believed in Hindu-Muslim unity. He sacrificed his life for the freedom of India and remains a symbol of unity and courage.

SUKHDEV THAPAR

Sukhdev was born on 15 May 1907 in Ludhiana, Punjab. He was deeply influenced by revolutionary ideas. He worked closely with Bhagat Singh and Rajguru. Sukhdev played an important role in organizing youth for the freedom movement. He was arrested and executed by the British for his revolutionary activities. His sacrifice strengthened the spirit of resistance against colonial rule.

SHIVRAM RAJGURU

Shivaram Rajguru was born on 24 August 1908 in Kheda, Maharashtra. His father was Harinarayan Rajguru. He was skilled in shooting and joined the revolutionary movement. Rajguru assassinated British officer Saunders to protest injustice. He was arrested and executed along with Bhagat Singh and Sukhdev. His bravery and sacrifice made him a respected martyr of India’s freedom struggle.

MADAN MOHAN MALAVIYA

Madan Mohan Malaviya, known as “Mahamana Malaviya”, was born on 25 December 1861 in Allahabad. His father was Pandit Brijnath and his mother was Moona Devi. He was a great scholar and educationist. He founded Banaras Hindu University. Malaviya supported the freedom movement through education, leadership, and social reform, contributing greatly to nation building.

AUROBINDO GHOSH

Aurobindo Ghosh was born on 15 August 1872 in Kolkata. His father was Krishna Dhan Ghosh and his mother was Swarnalata Devi. He studied in England. Aurobindo was a revolutionary leader and later became a spiritual philosopher. His writings inspired nationalism and self-confidence among Indians. He played an important role in strengthening the freedom movement through ideas and vision.

GOPAL KRISHNA GOKHALE

Gopal Krishna Gokhale was born on 9 May 1866 in Maharashtra. His father was Krishna Rao Gokhale. He was a social reformer and freedom fighter. Gokhale believed in peaceful methods and constitutional reforms. He was a senior leader of the Indian National Congress and the political mentor of Mahatma Gandhi. His ideas influenced India’s freedom struggle deeply.

BEGUM HAZARAT MAHAL

Begum Hazrat Mahal was born in 1820 in Faizabad. She was the wife of Nawab Wajid Ali Shah. She played a leading role in the Revolt of 1857 from Lucknow. Begum Hazrat Mahal organized resistance against British forces and showed remarkable leadership. Her bravery proved that women played a powerful role in India’s struggle for independence.
