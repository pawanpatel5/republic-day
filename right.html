FUNDAMENTAL RIGHT

RIGHT TO Equality

The Right to Equality ensures that every citizen is treated equally before the law. It removes discrimination based on religion, race, caste or place of birth. This right promotes fairness, equal opportunities in public employment, and abolishes untouchability and titles, strengthening social justice in India

RIGHT TO FREEDOM

The Right to Freedom guarantees essential personal liberties such as freedom of speech and expression, assembly, association, movement, residence, and profession. It also protects life and personal liberty. These freedoms help individuals think independently, express opinions, and live without unnecessary interference from the state.

RIGHT AGAINST EXPLOITATION

The Right against Exploitation protects people from abuse and inhuman treatment. It strictly prohibits human trafficking, forced labor, and child labor in hazardous industries. This right safeguards vulnerable sections of society and ensures that no one is forced to work against their will.

RIGHT TO FREEDOM OF RELIGION

The Right to Freedom of Religion gives every citizen the freedom to practice, profess, and propagate any religion of their choice. It ensures religious tolerance and harmony in a diverse country like India. The state does not favor any religion and treats all religions equally.

CULTURAL AND EDUCATIONAL RIGHTS

Cultural and Educational Rights protect the interests of minorities by allowing them to preserve their language, culture, and traditions. Minorities can establish and manage their own educational institutions. This right promotes unity in diversity and ensures that cultural identities are respected.

RIGHT TO CONSTITUTIONAL REMEDIES

The Right to Constitutional Remedies allows citizens to approach the Supreme Court or High Courts if their Fundamental Rights are violated. Courts can issue writs to enforce these rights. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called this right the “heart and soul” of the Constitution.

SUMMARY

At the time of adoption, India had seven Fundamental Rights: Right to Equality, Right to Freedom, Right against Exploitation, Right to Freedom of Religion, Cultural and Educational Rights, Right to Constitutional Remedies, and Right to Property. Later, the Right to Property was removed from the list of Fundamental Rights by the 44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978 and made a legal right instead. This change was made because the property right often obstructed land reforms and development projects. Today, India has six Fundamental Rights that protect citizens’ freedom, equality, justice, and dignity.
